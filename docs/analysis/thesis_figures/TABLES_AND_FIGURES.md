## Captions and notes

**T1. LPBF experimental setup and fixed process parameters.** 

**T2. Experimental coupon matrix.** VED = P / (v h t) with h = 0.080 mm and t = 0.030 mm. ×2: the same condition was built on Jul 30 and Jul 31. Roles: calibration 9, held out in window 5, out of window examined 7, out of window blind 3 coupons.

**F1. Experimental process space and data roles.** Laser power against scan speed for the 18 process conditions (24 coupons). Marker shape and fill give the analysis role; ×2 marks the six conditions built on both July 30 and July 31. The shaded band is the 65–85 J/mm³ calibration window, thin lines are VED isolines and dotted lines show the campaign designs.

**T3. Recorded data channels and their roles.** 

**F2. Extraction of the experimental melt-pool dimensions.** (a) Coaxial frame 3298 of coupon 20260730:part_3 (160 W, 950 mm/s, 70.2 J/mm³), cropped from the 64 × 24 px image. (b) Pixels at or above 150 counts; the largest 8-connected component is the camera's melt pool. (c) Its external contour and minimum-area rectangle. (d) H and W are the rectangle's long and short sides, in µm at the nominal 8 µm/px. The frame is re-measured with the documented convention; its stored H × W is 11.1 × 9.1 px.

**F3. Observation, scoring and hierarchical aggregation.** (a) The path of a camera frame from synchronization to scoring; shaded steps exclude frames, and excluded frames are recorded rather than deleted. (b) Squared log residuals are averaged from frame to scan track, coupon, process condition and analysis group, each level as an unweighted mean, so that densely sampled tracks, coupons or conditions receive no extra weight.

**T4. 316L material properties used by the model.** Phase-dependent first-pass engineering values; they were fixed and not calibrated. The directional term (η) multiplies the liquid conductivity along the scan by (1 + η); η = 0 in the selected state.

**F4. Reduced-order thermal model.** (a) Schematic of the moving computational window (99 × 99 × 21 cubic cells of 16 µm; depth drawn twice its scale) with the Gaussian volumetric source, boundary treatments and the liquidus footprint. (b) Simulated top-surface temperature of the selected state at frame 3298 of 20260730:part_3 (70.2 J/mm³) with the liquidus contour of the laser-associated component (white) and its minimum-area rectangle (H = 163 µm, W = 53 µm); + marks the beam centre.

**T5. Final numerical-model configuration.** Material properties are listed separately. Absorptivity and deposition depth share one calibration coordinate, t.

**T6. Calibration parameters and bounds.** Selected: Candidate 1, the calibrated state. Increasing t raises α and shortens δ. The boiling-regulator strength (b = 0.5) and all other quantities were fixed and are not calibration coordinates.

**F5. Calibration, data freeze and evaluation sequence.** Calibration-design and held-out coupons were separated by process condition before any fitting. The held-out coupons were not compared with the model until the selection freeze; the out-of-window calibration-design coupons had been examined during model development (dashed arrow). The calibration search combined a predefined design of anchor and Halton points with confirmation candidates proposed by a quadratic surrogate, and the candidate with the lowest measured objective was selected.

**F6. Melt-pool footprints over process space.** Each pair of boxes sits at its process condition, with replicate coupons pooled. The orange outline is the camera's median melt-pool footprint and the blue box the calibrated model's, both drawn to one size scale with H horizontal and W vertical. The shaded band marks the calibration window and the thin lines are lines of constant volumetric energy density.

**T7. Repeatability of the July replicate conditions.** Camera coupon medians of the scored frames. Δ = 100 ln(Jul 31 / Jul 30); 95% intervals from 4,000 track-bootstrap resamples within each coupon. Mean |ΔH| = 0.5%, mean |ΔW| = 0.7%; no interval excludes zero.

**T8. Selected calibration state.** Both on the 9 calibration coupons. Candidate 1 lies at the lower bounds of t and η; c_k is interior. The near-best set (J ≤ 1.10 J_min) contains only these two candidates.

**F7. Calibration candidates ranked by the measured objective.** Measured objective J on the calibration coupons for every directly evaluated parameter state, split into its H term (dark) and W term (light); the columns give each state's calibration coordinates. Anchor and Halton points form the predefined search design, and Candidates 1 and 2 are the confirmation states proposed by the quadratic surrogate. The dashed line marks the predefined near-best limit relative to the lowest J.

**F8. Observed and predicted melt-pool dimensions in the calibration window.** Condition medians of camera and model H (a) and W (b) for the five calibration conditions; model medians over frames with a predicted pool. Solid line: 1:1; dashed line: pooled median ratio. Condition ratios range from 1.69 to 1.96 for H and from 0.73 to 0.88 for W.

**T9. Calibration and held-out performance.** Errors are hierarchical log-RMSEs over the group's conditions; ratios are medians of the frame ratios, with frames without a pool scored as H = W = 0 (1 px floor). Brackets: 95% percentile bootstrap over coupons within the group (single coupon: over tracks). Model sizes are medians of the frames with a pool. Predefined criterion V ≤ 1.25 for H and W: met for both.

**F9. Calibration and in-window held-out comparison.** (a) Pooled model-to-camera size ratios for the calibration and in-window held-out groups, with bootstrap intervals; tick marks are individual coupons. The model is longer and narrower than the camera footprint, by nearly the same factor in both groups. (b) Held-out error divided by calibration error, V, for H and W, with bootstrap intervals. Both pooled values lie close to equal error and inside the predefined pass criterion V ≤ 1.25. The grey square is the in-window held-out coupon from the speed-sweep campaign, shown alone.

**F10. Model-to-camera ratios against volumetric energy density.** Coupon median ratios of H (a) and W (b) on a log scale, with 95% track-bootstrap intervals; marker shape gives the data role and the shaded band the calibration window. Frames without a predicted pool are scored as H = W = 0 (1 px floor); they make up 53.5% of the scored frames at 41.7 J/mm³.

**T10. Out-of-window performance by process condition.** Each out-of-window condition is one coupon. Ratios are medians of the frame ratios (model / camera); intervals from 4,000 track-bootstrap resamples (single coupon) or coupon-bootstrap resamples (groups). 'Examined' coupons were compared with the model during development; 'blind' coupons were first compared after the freeze. Spearman rank correlation of the coupon median log residual with VED: ρ = 0.976 (H), 0.964 (W). Below the window, H is still larger than the camera's for 4 of 6 coupons; W is smaller for all 6.

**F11. Sensitivity of melt-pool size to the process parameters.** Fitted percentage change in melt-pool length H, width W and shape H/W for a one-percent increase in laser power (a), scan speed (b) and volumetric energy density (c), from log-log regressions over the coupon medians, for the camera (orange) and the calibrated model (blue); lines are coupon-bootstrap intervals. Camera and model change in the same direction, but the model responds several times more strongly in both length and width. The camera's shape barely changes with energy density, whereas the model's elongates. Leaving out the lowest-energy coupon does not change these conclusions.

**F12. Observation operators compared.** (a–c) One scored camera frame per energy-density level, with the camera threshold-150 outline (orange), the model liquidus outline (blue) and the simulated-camera threshold-150 outline (aqua; 850 nm, uniform emissivity, PSF 0 px, T150 = 1950 K) in camera pixels. (d–f) Group medians of the H, W and H/W ratios to the camera for the liquidus and the simulated camera (PSF 0 and 2 px). In the calibration group the H/W ratio is 2.34 for the liquidus and 1.03 for the simulated camera; simulated-camera coverage outside the window is 72% (PSF 0) and 69% (PSF 2).

**F13. Camera thresholds and model isotherms.** One scored frame of a calibration coupon, seen by the camera and by the calibrated model. (a) Camera melt-pool outlines at increasing brightness thresholds; the thick outline is the 150-count threshold used for scoring. (b) Camera H and W at each threshold, with H/W below. (c) Model outlines at increasing isotherms on the simulated top surface; the thick outline is the 1673 K liquidus used for scoring, the dashed frame marks the camera's field of view, and isotherms that leave the model window are not drawn. (d) Model H and W at each isotherm. Raising the threshold shrinks the camera footprint while it stays nearly round, whereas every model isotherm is more elongated than any camera outline, so changing the level does not bring the two shapes together.
